# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want participation around Iter to be constructive, respectful, and useful for people with different levels of experience and different technical backgrounds.

## Expected behavior

Examples of positive participation include:

- discussing ideas and technical trade-offs respectfully;
- giving specific, actionable feedback;
- welcoming questions and different points of view;
- focusing criticism on ideas, code, documentation, and behavior rather than people;
- respecting privacy, security, and the public/private boundaries of the Iter project.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following is not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or discriminatory abuse;
- deliberate disruption, spam, or impersonation;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- posting credentials, tokens, private Iter source code, internal infrastructure details, or other material that should not be public;
- knowingly misrepresenting unofficial work as an official Iter release or product.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to public Iter project spaces, including issues, pull requests, discussions, and other community interactions connected to this repository.

## Reporting

If you see a problem, use the repository's moderation and reporting tools. For security vulnerabilities, follow `SECURITY.md` instead of publishing sensitive details in a public issue.

Project maintainers may remove content or restrict participation when necessary to keep the project spaces safe, useful, and on topic.
